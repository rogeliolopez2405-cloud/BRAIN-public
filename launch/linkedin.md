I've published the first public architecture release of something I've been building for a
while. It's called BRAIN — Belief, Reality, Authority, Intelligence, Navigation.

The problem it comes from is simple to state. AI systems can be genuinely intelligent and still
have no continuity, because the continuity lives in the conversation, the model, the agent or
the process. When any of those ends, the world ends with it — and a person becomes the memory,
re-explaining the same situation every session.

BRAIN explores a different separation: the intelligence can be replaced while the persistent
world, its evidence, its authority and its verification remain.

The principle underneath it:

Intelligence proposes.
Evidence establishes.
Authority decides.
Reality verifies.

Four steps that are usually collapsed into one. A model suggesting something isn't evidence for
it. Evidence establishing what a system does isn't a decision about what may happen. A decision
isn't a verification — something has to actually happen and be observed.

This is a limited public architecture release, not the full private implementation. Its
classification is GENERAL_ARCHITECTURE_CANDIDATE: it passed my own architecture gate and has
not been validated by anyone else. The validation results published are internally produced. I
have also published five known limitations, deliberately, including the ones I can't currently
solve.

If you work in AI agents, distributed systems, reliability, robotics, AI infrastructure or
persistent state, I would rather hear where this architecture fails than hear that you like it.
